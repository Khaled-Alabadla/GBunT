# Coffee Types

## Arabica

Arabica is the most famous and high-quality coffee bean type. Grows in high-altitude regions (1000-2000 meters above sea level) and requires moderate temperatures. Characterized by smooth, complex flavors with medium acidity and notes of fruits and flowers. Famous Arabica varieties include: Typica, Bourbon, Caturra, and Geisha.

## Robusta

Robusta is the second most common type. Grows in low-altitude areas and resists diseases and high temperatures. Contains higher caffeine content (2.2-2.7%) compared to Arabica (1.2-1.5%). Characterized by stronger, more bitter flavors with notes of chocolate and nuts. Often used in espresso and instant coffee.

## Liberica

A rare type grown in Malaysia and the Philippines. Characterized by unique flavors resembling tropical fruits and smoke. Beans are asymmetrical in shape.

## Excelsa

A subtype of Liberica, grown in Southeast Asia. Characterized by light flavors with high acidity and fruity notes.

# Brewing Methods

## Espresso

The classic Italian brewing method. Hot water (90-96°C) passes under high pressure (9 bar) through finely ground coffee for 25-30 seconds. Result: concentrated coffee with a golden crema layer. The base for latte, cappuccino, and americano.

## French Press

Simple immersion method. Coarsely ground coffee is added to hot water (93-96°C) and left for 4 minutes, then the plunger is pressed to separate the coffee. Produces rich, full-bodied coffee with clear flavor.

## Chemex

Manual pour-over method using thick paper filter. Medium-fine grind, water (92-96°C) is poured slowly. Produces clean, pure coffee with distinct flavors.

## Moka Pot

Italian home coffee maker. Water boils at the bottom, steam pushes water through coffee to the top. Produces strong coffee similar to espresso but without crema.

## Cold Brew

Cold coffee. Coarsely ground coffee is steeped in cold water for 12-24 hours. Produces smooth coffee without acidity with higher caffeine content.

## Pour Over Drip

Manual drip method using a cone and filter. Water (92-96°C) is poured circularly over medium-fine ground coffee. Produces balanced, clean coffee.

## Aeropress

Hybrid method combining immersion and pressure. Medium-fine grind, hot water (85-90°C) is added then air is pressed. Fast and versatile.

# Origins

## Ethiopia

The birthplace of coffee. Varieties include: Yirgacheffe (berry and lemon notes), Sidamo (tropical fruits), Guji (tea and fruits), Harrar (spices and honey). Characterized by high acidity and complex flavors.

## Colombia

Largest Arabica producer. Characterized by balanced flavors with medium acidity, notes of caramel, chocolate, and fruits. Famous regions: Huila, Nariño, Tolima.

## Brazil

World's largest coffee producer. Produces both Arabica and Robusta. Characterized by light flavors with low acidity, notes of vanilla, chocolate, and nuts.

## Yemen

Origin of Arab coffee. Varieties include: Matali (honey and spices), Haimi (fruits), Nakha (saffron). Characterized by complex, rich flavors.

## Kenya

Characterized by high acidity and citrus fruit notes (orange, berries). Famous varieties: SL28 and SL34. Washed processing produces clean coffee.

## Guatemala

Characterized by dark chocolate and spice notes. Regions: Antigua (volcanic), Atitlán (lake). Dry processing produces rich coffee.

## Costa Rica

Characterized by clean, balanced flavors. Varieties include: Caturra, Villalobia, Honey (honey processed).

## Indonesia

Characterized by earthy, tobacco, and dark chocolate notes. Includes Sumatra (Giling Basah), Java, Sulawesi.

# Roasting Levels

## Light Roast

Temperature 196-205°C. Beans are light brown, no oil on surface. Characterized by high acidity and fruit, flower notes. Preserves the original coffee flavor.

## Medium Roast

Temperature 210-219°C. Beans are medium brown, slight oil. Characterized by medium acidity and balanced flavors with caramel and nut notes. Most popular.

## Medium-Dark Roast

Temperature 225-230°C. Beans are dark brown, visible oil. Characterized by low acidity and rich flavors with dark chocolate notes.

## Dark Roast

Temperature 240-250°C. Beans are black, heavy oil. Characterized by very low acidity and strong flavors with smoke and burnt caramel notes. Suitable for espresso.

# Coffee Drinks

## Espresso

30ml of concentrated coffee with crema. The base for all Italian drinks.

## Americano

Espresso + hot water. Ratio usually 1:4. Light coffee similar to drip coffee.

## Cappuccino

Espresso + steamed milk + milk foam in equal ratio (1:1:1). Thick, stable foam.

## Caffè Latte

Espresso + steamed milk + light foam. Ratio 1:3:1. Smooth, milk-rich coffee.

## Flat White

Double espresso + steamed milk with fine foam. Strong coffee with milk flavor.

## Macchiato

Espresso + spoonful of milk foam. "Stained" in Italian.

## Mochaccino

Espresso + chocolate + steamed milk + foam. Sweet like hot chocolate.

## Iced Latte

Espresso + cold milk + ice. Refreshing summer coffee.

## Affogato

Hot espresso over vanilla ice cream. Classic Italian dessert.

# Arabic and Turkish Coffee

## Arabic Coffee (Gahwa)

Brewed in dallah (copper pot). Lightly roasted, coarsely ground coffee. Cardamom and saffron sometimes added. Served with dates. Important part of Arabic hospitality.

## Turkish Coffee (Türk Kahvesi)

Brewed in small copper pot (Cezve). Very fine powder-like grind. Boiled with sugar several times. Served with grounds in cup. Used for fortune telling.

# Health and Caffeine

## Caffeine

Natural stimulant found in coffee. Average cup contains 95mg caffeine. Espresso: 63mg per shot. Cold brew: 200mg per cup.

## Health Benefits

- Improves focus and alertness
- Contains antioxidants
- May reduce risk of heart disease and type 2 diabetes
- Improves athletic performance
- May reduce risk of certain cancers

## Side Effects

- Insomnia and sleep disturbance
- Anxiety and stress
- Increased heart rate
- Temporary blood pressure increase
- Addiction with excessive consumption

## Recommended Daily Limit

Healthy limit: 400mg daily for adults (about 4 cups). Pregnancy: 200mg daily. Children: less than 100mg.

# Coffee Storage

## Golden Rules

- Store coffee in airtight container
- Store in cool, dry place
- Avoid light, humidity, and heat
- Don't store in fridge (humidity ruins flavor)

## Optimal Duration

- Whole beans: 2-4 weeks after opening
- Ground coffee: 1-2 weeks after grinding
- Freshly roasted: consume within a month

## Best Place

Kitchen cupboard away from stove and sun. Ceramic or opaque glass container.

## 1) Introduction

Coffee is not only a beverage across the Arab world—it is a social language.  
In **the Levant (Sham), Egypt, and the Gulf**, coffee traditions combine:

- hospitality rituals,
- finely tuned flavor preferences (sweetness and spices),
- distinct serving styles,
- and generation-to-generation methods.

While many households share a common base idea—**warm spiced coffee served to guests**—each country develops its own “signature” through:

- spice choices (especially **cardamom**),
- sweetness approach (plain vs sweet vs very sweet),
- thickness and texture (how finely ground the coffee is),
- and serving behavior (how many pours, cup size, and pace).

---

## 2) Coffee Fundamentals: From Bean to Brew

Before focusing on regional traditions, it helps to understand the basic levers that shape coffee flavor:

### 2.1 Extraction

Extraction is how water pulls flavors from grounds. It depends on:

- grind size,
- brew time,
- water temperature,
- agitation (stirring / simmer),
- and ratio (coffee to water).

### 2.2 Body & Texture

In traditional Arab coffee, texture matters. If the coffee is very fine and heated in a controlled way, it can produce:

- a heavier body,
- more sediment,
- and sometimes a foam layer (especially in cezve-style brewing).

### 2.3 Aroma & Spices

Spices do more than add flavor:

- they enhance perceived sweetness,
- soften harsh bitterness,
- and create a layered aroma that stays longer.

---

## 3 “Arab Coffee” Identity: What People Usually Mean

“Arab coffee” is often used as a broad label. In practice, the region frequently includes:

- **Spiced, cardamom-forward coffee** (often sweetened)
- **Very fine-ground brewing** (traditional pot approach)
- **Small servings** with strong aroma emphasis

The exact version depends on:

- country customs,
- family preference,
- local availability of spices,
- and whether the coffee is for daily home drinking or formal hospitality.

---

## 4) Core Preparation Styles Across the Region

Across Sham, Egypt, and the Gulf, you’ll commonly see these “families” of coffee preparation:

### 4.1 Cardamom-Style Spiced Coffee (Hospitality Core)

- finely ground coffee
- water
- cardamom (sometimes freshly crushed or powdered)
- sugar (often adjusted)
- gentle simmer, then service

### 4.2 Turkish-Style Coffee (Cezve-Style)

- extremely fine grind
- foam formation is valued
- typically brewed in a small pot
- served with careful pouring

### 4.3 Coffee House / Café Styles (Modern Influence)

In cities, the café ecosystem introduces:

- espresso-based drinks
- lighter roasts
- milk drinks
- filter/pour-over for specialty enthusiasts

Yet, traditional spiced coffee remains the hospitality “default” in many homes.

## 6 Ingredients: Coffee, Water, Sugar, Cardamom, and More

### 6.1 Coffee

Traditionally, many households use blends designed to:

- handle stronger spices,
- deliver deeper flavor at darker roasts,
- maintain body when ground very fine.

In modern times, some families use single-origin or specialty beans—but the traditional preference remains common.

### 6.2 Water Quality

Water affects taste significantly. Softer water can make flavors feel cleaner; hard water can create dullness or scale.

### 6.3 Sugar

Sugar ranges from:

- minimal sweetness,
- to moderate,
- to very sweet “guest cup” traditions.

Sugar timing may also vary:

- added during brewing or dissolved before serving,
- then adjusted for each household.

### 6.4 Cardamom (The Signature Spice)

Cardamom contributes:

- warm, floral notes,
- a gentle sweetness perception,
- and a smoother cup.

Some households crush pods fresh; others rely on ground cardamom.

### 6.5 Other Additions (Occasional or Region-Specific)

You may encounter:

- cinnamon,
- cloves,
- saffron (rare, special occasions),
- orange blossom or rose water (more dessert-like contexts).

---

## 7) Roasting & Grinding: How Texture Shapes the Cup

### 7.1 Roasting

- Darker roasts often suit spiced hospitality coffee (stronger baseline flavor).
- Medium roasts can work well if sweetness and spices are carefully balanced.

### 7.2 Grinding

- Cardamom-style traditional coffee is frequently very fine.
- Turkish-style coffee is often powder-like fineness.

Too coarse → weak extraction and watery taste.  
Too fine or too long → excessive bitterness and heavy sediment.

---

## 8) Service Culture & Etiquette

In this region, coffee service often communicates:

- respect,
- welcome,
- and social acceptance.

Common patterns include:

- offering coffee early in visits,
- returning to guests with “the second cup” depending on customs,
- serving in small portions to preserve ritual intensity.

### Guest behavior often includes:

- receiving the cup with appreciation,
- sipping slowly to enjoy aroma and texture.

---

## 9 Common Sweetness Levels and How They Differ by Country

There isn’t one universal “sweetness rule.” However, you can expect a spectrum:

- **Some households prefer lightly sweet** coffee to let cardamom and coffee bitterness blend.
- **Others prefer a noticeably sweet cup** where cardamom becomes aromatic harmony.
- **Formal hospitality** often leans toward controlled sweetness—never too harsh.

If you compare countries, you’ll see:

- different default sugar levels,
- and different “who controls the sweetness” norms (family vs guest).

---

## 10 Step-by-Step: How to Make Traditional Spiced Coffee at Home

This is a general method adaptable to local preferences.

### Ingredients (Adjust per Household)

- finely ground coffee
- water
- cardamom
- sugar (optional)

### Steps

1. **Prepare cups** first (coffee is best served immediately).
2. In a small pot, combine water and coffee grounds.
3. Add cardamom and sugar to taste (or to the household default).
4. Heat on low-to-medium until it reaches a gentle simmer.
5. Watch for rising foam or thickening; avoid a hard boil.
6. Pour carefully into small cups.
7. Serve immediately while aroma is strongest.

# 11 Country Sections

## 11.1 Syria

### Coffee Role in Daily Life

Syrians often treat coffee as a social centerpiece—especially in family visits and neighborhood gatherings. The coffee cup is commonly associated with conversation and hospitality.

### Most Common Traditional Styles

- **Cardamom-forward spiced coffee**
- **Turkish-style coffee** in many urban areas
- **Café coffee** increasing in larger cities

### Typical Flavor Direction

- warm aromatic cardamom
- moderate to sweet profiles depending on household
- thicker texture from fine grinding

### How It’s Usually Served

- small cups
- quick service after brewing
- sometimes a second offering during the visit

### What Syrians Usually Do “By Default”

- use fine coffee suitable for a thicker, aromatic cup
- adjust sweetness depending on the occasion (home vs guests)

# Gulf Cooperation Countries

### Coffee as Hospitality Symbol

In Saudi Arabia, coffee is strongly connected to formal welcome. Serving coffee correctly is a sign of respect, and it’s central to many social occasions.

### Signature Style

- **Gulf Arabian coffee**: spiced, often cardamom-based, commonly served with sugar adjustments.

### Typical Characteristics

- fragrant aroma (cardamom)
- sweet balance (not always heavy; often “pleasantly sweet”)
- presentation in small cups
- long-spout pot is common for serving
